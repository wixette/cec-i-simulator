# v0.0.5: disks and files

**Status:** released 2026-11-22 as a pre-release ([the release](https://github.com/wixette/cec-i-simulator/releases/tag/v0.0.5)).

The CEC-I with a disk drive, as many schools had it: Apple II disks the visitor brings boot and run, and what they write can be taken away. Programs are saved with a name. LOGO procedures can be opened from a file and saved to one. The Text editor saves its text on a DOS disk.

Agreed with the owner, who also cut the following from the earlier plan:
- **The cassette recorder.** The machine was not sold with one, and few used one with it. It would weigh on the 3D view and the page, and the TRS-80 simulator is where a recorder fits.
- **Saving the Text editor's text without a disk.** The machine had no natural way to do it; with DOS on a disk it saves the period's way (P4).

Related documents: the machine's decisions ([decisions.md](decisions.md), D15), the page's ([../docs/ui-design.md](../docs/ui-design.md), U14).

## 1. The disk drive (P1)

The board has its own floppy interface (U39-U44, the 驱动器 connector J4 at the back), compatible with Apple's Disk II, in slot 6, for one 5.25-inch drive (the software reference, part 3, 1.2.1; the user manual, ch. 6: `S6`, `D1`). Its boot code is in U35 at $C600. The system ROM looks for a disk at every cold start: it starts the motor, steps the head to track 0 and waits about a second for an address field (U7 $C800-$C88E); then it boots the disk at $C600, or goes on to BASIC. `PR#6` boots it at any time.

- **A virtual drive, no 3D model:** the drive is behind J4, out of the picture. The 3D view does not change.
- **The controller as the hardware is:** the soft switches $C0E0-$C0EF (the four phases, the motor, the drive select, Q6 and Q7), the head on quarter tracks, the disk as a stream of bits under the head at 4 cycles a bit, the data latch, writing, write protect. Copy-protected originals (WOZ) work as far as their bits are kept.
- **Formats in:**
  - `.dsk` and `.do`: 140 KB, DOS 3.3 order;
  - `.po`: 140 KB, ProDOS order (a `.dsk` holding ProDOS order is recognised);
  - `.nib`: raw tracks;
  - `.woz`: versions 1 and 2.
  
  Not `.2mg` or 3.5-inch disks: the machine had no such drive.
- **Out:** *Save the disk image* gives back what was loaded, with what was written: a sector image as `.dsk`/`.po`, the others as `.woz`.
- **Limits, said on the page:** 64 KB and no auxiliary RAM, so software that needs 128 KB, 80 columns or double hi-res does not run. No disk software is hosted here (decisions.md): the visitor brings their own.

### On the page (U14)

- **Programs ▸ Disk:**
  - *Insert a disk image…*: after inserting it, a dialog offers *Start from the disk* (as the manual starts it: switched off and on, the disk in the drive) or *Just insert it* (the machine as it is, for `PR#6` or `CATALOG` later);
  - *Insert a blank disk*, to `INIT` from a DOS of the visitor's own;
  - *Save the disk image*;
  - *Eject*.
- **The drive on the helper strip,** always there, a second way to the Disk menu (a control that came and went would not be one to rely on): the disk's name, or *No disk*, and the drive's lamp, lit red while the motor turns, as the drive's own lamp (also while the ROM looks for a disk at power-on).
- **A dropped file:** a disk image dropped on the page is inserted, as *Insert*.

## 2. Saving a program with a name (P2)

*Save the program as a file* asks for a name, offering the program's first `REM` line, or `PROGRAM`, as the name. The file is `<name>.bas`.

## 3. LOGO procedures from a file (P3)

*Open a file* takes a `.logo` file of procedures too: LOGO is started, and the procedures are typed in through its editor (`TO`, then Ctrl-C), as Kaleidoscope is. *Save the program*, while LOGO runs, types LOGO's own `PO ALL`, as a LOGO user would, and reads what LOGO prints as it prints it (its output routine, $1D0F, where CSW points while LOGO runs): the procedures, clean, saved as `<name>.logo`, named after the first.

## 4. The Text editor saves on a DOS disk (P4)

As programs of the time kept their data: with DOS 3.3 started from a disk (the visitor's own: none is hosted), the editor's BASIC part offers *L: load a text from the disk* at its start and *S: save it on the disk* after Quit, with DOS's own `BSAVE` and `BLOAD` of $6600 to the text's end (the editor's state with its text: the cursor comes back where it was). DOS is known by its entry at $3D0 (`JMP $9DBF`). Its errors (no such file, write protected, not formatted, full) are said in plain words. Without DOS, the editor says how to save and offers no disk. Its text ends at $95FF, below DOS's $9600, so the two share memory as they did; Ctrl-Reset, which types it in, keeps DOS.

## 6. Checks

- **Unit tests:**
  - nibblizing and back, for every sector of both orders;
  - WOZ in and out;
  - a disk of our own, made in the test (no Apple software), booted by the machine's own ROM at a cold start and by `PR#6`, its boot code loading more sectors through the ROM's read routine;
  - written sectors read back;
  - an empty drive still starts BASIC;
  - LOGO procedures typed in from a file and read back by `PO ALL`, the same (`tests/files.test.js`);
  - the Text editor without DOS: it says how to save, and offers no disk (`tests/programs.test.js`).
- **Browser checks** (`tests/browser/disk.mjs`): a disk dropped on the page, the dialog, the lamp, our disk booted, Save the disk image, Eject, a blank disk; Save with a name.
- **By hand, not committed:** the owner's own DOS 3.3 System Master boots, `CATALOG` lists it, and `INIT` formats a blank disk that, saved and loaded again, boots and runs its `HELLO`. On it, the Text editor saves a text (`B 004 MY TEXT` in the catalog), loads it back typed in afresh, says a missing name, and the saved disk image lists the file on a fresh machine.
